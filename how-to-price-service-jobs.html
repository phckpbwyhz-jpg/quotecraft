How to price a service job without guessing

A profitable quote starts with the cost of doing the work, not with what a competitor happens to charge. Use five inputs: labor, materials, direct job costs, overhead and target profit margin.

1. Calculate direct labor

Estimate the hours the job should take and multiply by your true labor cost per hour. If you are the owner doing the work, your labor still has a cost. Treating your own time as free makes a job look more profitable than it is.

Labor cost = labor hours × labor cost per hour

2. Add materials and direct costs

Include consumables, job-specific materials, fuel, disposal, parking, subcontractors and other costs that exist because you accepted the job.

3. Allocate overhead

Insurance, software, tools, phones, advertising, bookkeeping, vehicle costs and admin time have to be paid by the jobs you sell. Use a consistent allocation method instead of pretending overhead is zero.

4. Price for margin, not just markup

Once you know total job cost, convert your target profit margin into a selling price.

Selling price = total job cost ÷ (1 − target margin)

If total job cost is $100 and you want a 30% margin, the selling price is about $142.86. Charging $130 is a 30% markup on cost, but only about a 23% margin on revenue.

5. Check the result against reality

Your calculated minimum profitable price still needs a market sanity check. If the market will not support the number, the answer is usually to lower job cost, improve efficiency, change the offer or target a better customer segment—not to ignore the math.
