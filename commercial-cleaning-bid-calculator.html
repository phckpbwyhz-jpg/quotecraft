What a commercial cleaning bid should cover

  • Loaded labor: wages plus payroll taxes, workers' compensation and other labor burden.
  • Supplies: chemicals, liners, paper goods you provide, laundry and consumables.
  • Travel and access: drive time, parking, keys, security or unusual access requirements.
  • Overhead: insurance, vehicles, software, admin, equipment and management time.
  • Profit: enough room above total job cost to absorb mistakes, callbacks and growth.

Why the lowest bid can still lose money

A monthly contract can look attractive while loaded labor consumes most of the selling price. QuoteCraft's cleaning calculator checks both a full-cost gross-margin floor and a separate loaded-labor-share floor, then uses the stricter result as the recommended minimum quote.

That makes it useful for recurring office cleaning, janitorial contracts, medical-office cleaning, retail spaces, move-in/move-out work and other labor-heavy accounts.

Turn a visit price into a monthly contract

For recurring work, enter visits per week and the calculator projects average monthly revenue, monthly gross profit and annual revenue using 4.333 weeks per month. After the first several visits, compare estimated hours against actual production time and reprice future bids when the estimate was wrong.

Commercial cleaning bid formula

Loaded labor = crew hours × loaded hourly labor rate.

Total visit cost = loaded labor + supplies + travel + allocated overhead.

Margin price floor = total visit cost ÷ (1 − target gross margin).

The calculator also pressure-tests the quote against your chosen maximum labor share so a labor-heavy account does not pass the margin check while still leaving too little room for the rest of the business.
